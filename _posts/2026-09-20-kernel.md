I restarted my local model project, Turing and Samantha, and learned a half-billion-parameter model memorizes style but can't learn facts. So I pivoted to looking answers up in my notes through brain, and the score jumped from one correct to 14, extraction shortcuts at 100 percent. I fixed brain's Worker, widened its coverage, and noted the small Qwen model as the hardware ceiling. Grilling it turned up funny bugs: a blocked project returned censorship articles and model questions got food donations, so I fixed the matching and added regression tests. A new Craigslist skill posts to Craigslist, Marketplace, and Kijiji; I tested it with a Canon printer on all three.

Then Joshua Tree took over: a kernel with real interrupts, paging, task switching, disks, filesystems, and a file browser, and a landing demo that boots the OS live in the browser. A polish pass brought 20 fixes: a Mac-style clock, redrawn icons, a Notes editor, a 1984-style hello on boot, the Mojave palette, a real park service wallpaper, and an Apple menu. Calendar came next with persistent events and 73,049 dates verified, plus Settings toggles, a real Trash, a glass menu bar, and a landing page with a progress graph and parallax.

One Saturday I pushed v56 through v72 in one loop: Mail, Contacts, Calculator, day and night wallpaper, weather particles, and real location lookup so it stops claiming Vancouver. The notification panel's wrong font was the cursor smoothing the text underneath; the dock's stuck magnify was two stacked problems, one a hit test off by half a tile since v15; and the weather had been asking about the wrong city all along. The network driver had run one packet behind since v7, hidden for sixty versions until weather made two requests back to back. I added a real syscall gate, clean teardown for user tasks, VMware mouse support, full documentation, and CI on every push.

That Monday moving the landing header broke the boot logo's position until I traced it. I stopped CI failing on network hiccups, cut the idle tour to three apps, reorganized the tools, and refused to fake multi-window in JavaScript. The business thesis became a skill: the OS stays free, the money comes from hardware, a dev kit, voice, and consulting.

Next I fixed two security bugs, shipped a JPEG decoder, ported a second network driver, and wired 15 regression tests into CI. Multi-window arrived for real with click to focus and stacking, and trimming the page table trackers saved memory. I backfilled 19 missing tags with their GitHub releases and made tagging automatic. The landing page got mouse wheel scrolling in Apps and Launchpad, a typewriter headline, better heading contrast, a rounded demo, and no changelog. A supposed stale cache was a real bug, the static page never matched what the script drew, and I caught a subagent reporting local commits as pushed, so I verify before calling anything done.

Midweek I traced a purple boot logo to a drawing function that ignored the color it was given. I fixed garbage text in Notes and a half-working scroll wheel, and swapped leftover pink and purple for silver, black, and white. The tree wallpaper kept flashing on startup because my fix ran everywhere except the one frame that mattered. Codex joined the project with shared instructions and a review loop, and the landing page now draws its plans from the work queue. By Friday I merged the Apps folder redraw, shipped a release, added a live reload loop and recorded headless test runs, and flattened the landing icon, leaving the screen repainting on every input as the real blocker.

This weekend it got glossy tiles, real syscalls, graphs, lazy loading for the browser demo, and the back buffer. Then pinning Stocks to the dock set off a cascade: gibberish in the landing terminal, a header out of sync, red CI, random check failures, lag, and dead weather. I spent a morning on it, frustrated. Sunday was better: the icon, a Weather window, a save test, and the landing page done, six pieces left before 1.0. I restored the satellite wallpaper's color and swapped the landing's papery look for blue and teal, up for review. A new Pixelmator logo tool recreated my dad's Best Choice logo in six seconds.

Meanwhile Curvely got real Desmos parity: type a = 3 and a draggable slider updates every equation using it, plus hover tracing and implicit equations like circles, web then native, both submitted. Roost got a clearer page and non-overlapping map labels, Dream got a delete-all control, and The Contrarian and Trading For Canadians became book badges. Hormuz is new, an oil price tracker with live geopolitics signals and a glass map. Conveyer came back after a restart: I killed a second runner fighting the first, fixed eight skill bugs in belts, inserters, smelting, and fuel, added base memory and a curriculum, and made its monitor launch on login. Gato's voice recording got fixed, and Pwnlingo's Korean tiles read the right text, its Settings window opens in front, and sound defaults to off.

The portfolio wasn't loading its design tokens, so hairlines showed yellow until I linked the right file. Later I stripped it to plain text, no graphs or fades, cleaned its folders, promoted the approved Curbfind, Healstack, and Plain to the main list, and gave Joshua Tree its own line. Co-Stanza's demo now paints instantly. Sparkjar iOS cleared review after weeks stuck in the spam wave, and its web app got a fixed What's New modal, sign in under a profile menu, and infinite scroll. Two Workers fought over brain's domain, so the memory graph viewer moved to brain.heyitsmejosh.com/graph. The feature audit skill records video now, since stills hide animation bugs, and a repo cleanup counted 55 real repos and 2 forks. Lexly's precalc masterclass got Unit 1 worked examples and a printable copy.

My main drive hit 100 percent full, 128MB free and Mail blocked. The culprit was 19GB of Ollama models, half stale, so I deleted the unused ones and kept two. I borrowed a 2TB LaCie from my dad and moved models, Movies, and Music onto it, up to 27GB free. Lucarne's Mac rejection got an appeal: the support link had been briefly down, the thin-app pushback was wrong, and I fixed a real crash on closing the last window. Then the name: Fenster sounded too angry, Ikkuna faded, Janela was mid, and Madobe, Japanese for by the window, stuck. The rename went everywhere except the bundle ID and repo folder.

Pwnlingo had stalled for 23 hours, 1,033 cycles with zero XP, silent about why. I bounded its retries, fixed tile matching, dismissed stalled stories, and skipped blacklisted tracks; 46 tests pass. Onboarding went live on 11 apps. I committed the fleet to making money: eight apps moved to 99 cents, still free on the web, a money file in every repo, launch kits for all 23 apps, and Voxprint's paywall live. Product Hunt already had twelve launches live and two scheduled, so the next round starts from that list. The launch skill now works in Codex, and I installed the new ChatGPT app, since the old one couldn't reach its Chrome extension.
