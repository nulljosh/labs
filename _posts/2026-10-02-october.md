## Oct 1 to 3

Joshua Tree reached 2.0 with twenty-six apps in protected boxes. Bookrank is live on iPhone with natural voices and offline reading. All my apps are now 99 cents.

Epiphany maps load in 4 seconds, not 23. Premium is $1 and auto-trading is live. A stock rule I paper-traded, Double 7s, hit 77 percent. Talli got rejected over a broken test account and resubmitted. Notate is live on iPhone.

## Oct 4

A Pi 4B is on the way for real hardware. On the Pi, apps run in locked rooms, and Bookrank, Tonchi and Hikko show real data.

My idea app became Hikko (Hotaru was taken), with near-white paper and terracotta accent. iPhone and Mac 3.0 are in review, the Mac with terracotta buttons and sidebar.

Mailbag fixed Mac window focus, demo sign-in and the sorter; Gmail sign-in on Mac is blocked. Lexly became Tonchi, now 165 courses. Healstack got a web redesign and a privacy fix; iOS is live, Mac in review.

Turing shipped with file-picking answers, a new Mac icon and her own 1.5B model. Wrong picks fell under 10 per set; refusals fell to 2.6 percent.

## Oct 5

Joshua Tree's landing is live with a refreshed logo on Samantha's face.

Apple rejected seven apps under spam: Hamurapi, Windgate, Madobe, Mailbag, Curbfind, Plaintxt, Sidewise; Hikko got stale screenshots. Plaintxt got line numbers and a preview; Sidewise and Madobe got real features. All five resubmitted.

Epiphany's map search uses Places now; iPhone submitted. Curbfind shipped Near me and a smarter ranker; Apple rejected it and I replied.

NYC Survive's overnight loop made it winnable; iOS is live, macOS in review. A headless bot wins it in two hours.

Swept 15 Apple Notes into project roadmaps and emptied Notes.app. Nimble shipped spoken math and dropped "Answers" from its store name.

## Oct 6

The Pi 4 booted Joshua Tree at 1920x1080 after a cache flush fix. A Claude relay lets my Mac build the OS inside it.

## Oct 7

The Pi joined Wi-Fi; the chip has no login helper, so I wrote its WPA2 handshake and the green light blinks again.

Samantha answers on the real Pi using my API credit. A relay on my Mac picks a cheap or strong model per question, and she can read a shared Mac folder, read-only.

An agent wrote the Pi-side actions on its branch, so asking Samantha now prints a note and blinks the light. A safety check blocked a Codex mode letting anyone on the network run programs on my Mac. An overnight runner works branches only, $5 cap per run.

Next: split the kernel's giant C file, then HTTPS. Money comes from a Pi dev kit with a waitlist; the OS stays free.

## Oct 8

I wrote the Joshua Tree story as an eleven-chapter book with board photos. The Pi's first benchmarks came back, with Wi-Fi up in 6.4 seconds.

The Pi got a scientific calculator and a tiny language model. After a DNS fix it loaded heyitsmejosh.com over HTTPS, its first real web page, though it still can't run scripts.

I also found a hole: every Pi image carried my relay key. That fix is in review, with two more queued: the key crosses Wi-Fi in plain text, and the clock trusts an unchecked date header.

## Oct 9 to 10

I halved my custom skills and flashed the Pi card. I redesigned the signup email and fixed mobile signup. Relay encryption and clock checks still need hardware.

The landing refresh is live; a draft adds Pi screen sleep.

## Oct 11

I built xread, a digest of twenty AI accounts on X, and Plank can call AI models. Most web apps moved from Supabase to spark-api, my Cloudflare backend; passwords stayed behind, so everyone resets. Heyitsmejosh.com lapses on the 13th by choice, so the brand moves to jaybulb.com and about forty repos follow.

Goodreads shelves sit under my summaries. I called the Mac app's first layout hideous, so it became a Mail-style three-column window. When spark-api stopped recognising their sign-in, the installed apps fell back to the robot voice, and the fix was approved today. The voice meter sat on Checking, and native users shared one daily cap; now each gets one.

From chat, Turing can boot, type into, screenshot and stop Joshua Tree, and read its log. A real boot found a bug that could have killed another session's build machine, so she now stops only her own. I tested 109 of her older abilities and fixed 24. Her newest training run became the tool picker, better on four of five test sets with fewer leaks, and the iPhone app runs her tools too. Files on the Pi survive a reboot.

I started marketing Dad's Langley garage door business: 37 Instagram posts, a $4 trailer. The Katana shot needs a paid Higgsfield plan, so I used a cheap stand-in. A stock voice sounded wrong, so he records his own.

I cleared 93 YouTube Watch Later videos, deleted three retired projects, and moved my 69 saved X bookmarks into notes, then unbookmarked them.

Healstack is Intake, Voxprint is Notate, Sparkjar is Hikko and Lexly is Tonchi, in repos. Hacker News turned down Notate's Show HN, so I posted fourteen X launch threads. Eleven App Store listings moved to jaybulb.com where Apple allows. A Talli login bug on the new domain was a server allowlist I forgot.

Nimble got a big night: answers carry photos, news has thumbnails, and answers cite up to three sources. I also gave it a typeface, Nimble Sans, and a brand kit at nimble.jaybulb.com/brand. It's with Apple.

Tonight twenty Joshua Tree changes merged, covering typing into apps, Bluetooth, the SD card, Notes and Music, Samantha answering through a relay on my Mac, and a new logo. Then I ran it on a real Raspberry Pi 4 for the first time: it boots to a desktop, search works, the beep plays and Calendar opens. Bluetooth finds my mouse and the radio connects, but the mouse hangs up before pairing, and a second channel failed too, so that is the next fight. Tree Sans is live on the landing page; the OS-wide font is built, not pushed. The 3.0 release stays a draft until the board pass is done.

## Oct 12

Nimble only shows photos that match the answer's subject, credited to Wikipedia. Looping my 95-question exam burned the free AI quota, so answers fell back to Wikipedia. I said no to the $5 plan, so the app shows a quiet note that the free AI is resting.

Most of today went into the tool picker, the part of Turing that picks which abilities a question needs. It clears all three of my bars on dev, and the copy Joshua Tree carries is small C that stays out of plain chat, firing on 20 of 434 questions that needed no tool. The numbers are tuned to dev, so the sealed sets stay unread. Turing can hear me on the iPhone now, and Nimble has a voice loop for spoken questions.
