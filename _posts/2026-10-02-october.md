## Oct 1 to 2

Joshua Tree reached its 2.0 release. Twenty-six apps now run in protected boxes, and I fixed the last flaky tests. Bookrank is live on iPhone with natural voices, Goodreads import, offline reading and highlights. Plank shipped a big update, and Conveyer launched a second rocket. All my apps are now 99 cents.

Epiphany maps load in under 4 seconds now, down from 23. Premium plan is $1 and auto-trading is live. I tested a stock rule called Double 7s on paper money and it won 77 percent of the time.

Talli was rejected over a broken test account, so I fixed it and resubmitted. Notate went live on iPhone at 99 cents.

## Oct 3

Joshua Tree shipped six versions, with a 30-second intro, a cloned voice and Music and Movies apps. Turing answers as me as well as Samantha; I started moving the kernel to Raspberry Pi hardware. Windgate shows minutes in plain words, and Lexly resubmitted with Pre-Calc 12.
## Oct 4

A Raspberry Pi 4B is on the way to test Joshua Tree on real hardware. Apps on the Pi run in locked rooms, and Bookrank, Tonchi and Hikko show real data. Fixed Burrow cutting names, Stocks and Mail losing files, and the menu bar staying after Launchpad. The Launchpad grid centering is in CI. The roadmap now queues all 18 fleet apps for Launchpad.

My idea-catching app became Hikko (Hotaru was taken). Near-white paper with terracotta accent. iPhone and Mac 3.0 in review; Mac got terracotta buttons and sidebar. Blockframe says Blockframe everywhere now. New build to Apple.

Mailbag (renamed from Hagaki) is my mail-filing app. Fixed Mac window focus, demo sign-in and the sorter. Store not submitted (listing still Hagaki, 1.0 in review). Blocked: Gmail sign-in on Mac, Outlook parked.

Lexly became Tonchi (quick wit in Japanese). Content grew to 165 courses with learn-first cards. 32 language versions tested, 14 kept.

Healstack got a major web redesign: three tabs, white theme, blue accent. Fixed a privacy bug where new accounts inherited a hardcoded profile. Renaming it Intake: folder, repo, domain moved. iOS live; Mac in review.

Turing shipped with file-picking answers and new Mac icon, her own 1.5B model (850 MB). Wrong picks under 10 per set. v5.1 cut refusals to 2.6 percent.

## Oct 5

Turing shipped with guard. Joshua Tree landing is live with refreshed logo on Samantha's face; PR 425 merged with a design doc and its check. Loop stopped for the night.

Apple rejected seven apps under spam: Hamurapi, Windgate, Madobe, Mailbag, Curbfind, Plaintxt, Sidewise; Hikko got stale-screenshots. Four got replies, three got resubmitted, and Hikko went back with new screenshots. Plaintxt now has line numbers, Go to Line and preview. Sidewise rebuilt with Compare (same outlets), Share extension, widget. Madobe iOS and macOS got Mac Float (always on top, click-through), Pair pages, per-site rules, App Intents. All five resubmitted. Notate is live on iPhone; Tonchi, Talli, Intake, Curvely also rebuilding.
Brick went live with HousingFeed and RentCast. Bookrank shipped cover-fill from Open Library. Epiphany's map search now uses Places, then Overpass; iPhone submitted. Curbfind shipped Near me (closest city first) and a smarter deal ranker; Apple rejected it and I replied. Conveyer retired the beaten world;

Tonchi gained Greek and Polish courses and chess puzzles.

NYC Survive's overnight loop made the game winnable and shipped to App Review (iOS live since 2026-10-06, macOS in review). Recruits tie to beds and food, squads take orders, and night raids come unless you set PATROL. A headless bot plays it to victory in about two hours, and the landing has real playthrough video.

Alex, Joshua's best-friend character, now runs on Samantha's local model.

## Oct 5 to 6

Swept 15 Apple Notes into project roadmaps and emptied Notes.app. Nimble shipped spoken math ("nine plus ten"), a local "who made you" answer on web and Swift, bigger full-contrast answers on web and Mac HUD, and changed the store name from "Nimble Answers" to "Nimble".

Blockframe dropped toolbar title and tan for neutral gray. Notate, Talli, Plank fixed UI (padding, scroll, GitHub URL). Joshua Tree added desktop button on hero.

## Oct 6

The Raspberry Pi 4 arrived and booted Joshua Tree to a full 1920x1080 desktop by evening (the CPU cache flush was the fix). I wrote the USB driver in QEMU first, and a keyboard typed on the Pi the moment I swapped cables. A Claude relay lets my Mac help build the OS from inside it. The roadmap got pruned to ten items after research on what Linux and macOS have that we don't.

## Oct 7

The Pi joined Wi-Fi on the real board. The chip has no login helper, so I wrote the WPA2 handshake myself. The stall that ate the most time was one detail: the router wanted a block of association data repeated exactly in my reply. Then came a bad stretch of refused joins and no address, until a router reboot cleared it. The clock was slow because a lost DNS lookup sat waiting 20 seconds, so each try now waits 2 seconds and retries six times. The green light blink broke the keyboard, so it is off for now and the keyboard works again.

The big one: Samantha answers on the real Pi now, through my own API credit. A relay on my Mac picks a cheap or strong model for each question, so I never have to pick one. She can also read a shared folder on the Mac, read only. I asked her my IP and how strong the Wi-Fi was, and she gave me the Pi's real address and signal. The SD card kept dropping off the Mac, so the flash now waits for the card, mounts it and writes in one go.

An agent wrote the Pi-side action code on its own branch, so Samantha can print a note or blink the light. Not pushed yet, and the relay prompt does not know about those actions, so the test blink hasn't happened. I also had an overnight runner built for the repo, branches only, with a $5 cap per run, using Sonnet, Opus or Fable by task. The API spending limit is now $200.

I also wrote down the Mac gaps: Browser, Messages, FaceTime, Photos, Preview, Maps, System Settings as an app, App Store, Podcasts, and Control Center, battery and Focus in the menu bar. Next: split the kernel's one giant C file, then HTTPS on the Pi. The BearSSL library in the project is only five crypto files, so the full TLS client has to be vendored, and the plan is written into the roadmap. Money comes from a Pi dev kit with a waitlist already live, and the OS stays free. Weekly usage is at 93 percent, so the Pi action work waits for the Saturday reset.
