## Oct 6

The Raspberry Pi 4 arrived and booted Joshua Tree to a full 1920x1080 desktop by evening (the CPU cache flush was the fix). I wrote the USB driver in QEMU first, and a keyboard typed on the Pi the moment I swapped the cable. The Wi-Fi code boots but its output scrolls off screen. A Claude relay lets my Mac help build the OS from inside it. The roadmap pruned to ten items, backed by research on what Linux, macOS and others have that we don't.

## Oct 7

The Pi boots to a full 1920x1080 desktop with the satellite wallpaper, dock of eleven icons, window frame and working USB keyboard. The Wi-Fi chip reports its firmware version and the Pi's real MAC address, and a day of fixes went into how it replies and loads its firmware. Scans still need a test on real hardware. Reading failure lines off photos found the big bugs: the ARM core held in reset froze its own memory, chip RAM was 32 KB short, core addresses were guessed, and big frames have to go in pieces. The newest card carries the Burrow face and a Calendar page. Burrow's icon is a Finder-style split face in blue with near-black features. I drew a scribble Joshua tree logo in marker after seven rounds. The ARM mouse works in the emulator only and needs a real wired mouse. Eight fonts went into the kernel registry, and running-app dots under the dock icons are on the roadmap. Plan is Hacker News once the mouse works, drafted in docs/LAUNCH.md. The OS stays free; money comes from a Pi dev kit with a waitlist already live. We tried ten rounds of naming candidates; Joshua rated Null, Ampersand and Tilde okay, so Joshua Tree stays.
