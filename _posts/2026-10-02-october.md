## Oct 1 to 2

Joshua Tree reached its 2.0 release. Twenty-six apps now run in protected boxes, and I fixed the last flaky tests. Bookrank is live on iPhone with natural voices, Goodreads import, offline reading and highlights. All my apps are now 99 cents.

Epiphany maps load in under 4 seconds now, down from 23. Premium plan is $1 and auto-trading is live. I tested a stock rule called Double 7s on paper money and it won 77 percent of the time.

Talli was rejected over a broken test account, so I fixed it and resubmitted. Notate went live on iPhone at 99 cents.

## Oct 3

Joshua Tree shipped six versions with a 30-second intro and Music and Movies apps. I started moving the kernel to Raspberry Pi hardware. Windgate shows minutes in plain words, and Lexly resubmitted with Pre-Calc 12.
## Oct 4

A Raspberry Pi 4B is on the way to test Joshua Tree on real hardware. Apps on the Pi run in locked rooms, and Bookrank, Tonchi and Hikko show real data. Fixed Burrow names, Stocks and Mail losing files, and a stuck menu bar.

My idea-catching app became Hikko (Hotaru was taken). Near-white paper with terracotta accent. iPhone and Mac 3.0 in review; Mac got terracotta buttons and sidebar. Blockframe says Blockframe everywhere now. New build to Apple.

Mailbag is my mail app. Fixed Mac window focus, demo sign-in and the sorter. Blocked: Gmail sign-in on Mac.

Lexly became Tonchi. Content grew to 165 courses. 14 of 32 language versions kept.

Healstack got a web redesign. Fixed a privacy bug where new accounts inherited a hardcoded profile. Renaming it Intake: folder, repo, domain moved. iOS live; Mac in review.

Turing shipped with file-picking answers and new Mac icon, her own 1.5B model (850 MB). Wrong picks under 10 per set. v5.1 cut refusals to 2.6 percent.

## Oct 5

Joshua Tree landing is live with refreshed logo on Samantha's face; PR 425 merged with a design doc and its check.

Apple rejected seven apps under spam: Hamurapi, Windgate, Madobe, Mailbag, Curbfind, Plaintxt, Sidewise; Hikko got stale-screenshots. Four got replies and three were resubmitted. Plaintxt now has line numbers, Go to Line and preview. Sidewise rebuilt with Compare (same outlets), Share extension, widget. Madobe iOS and macOS got Mac Float (always on top, click-through), Pair pages, per-site rules, App Intents. All five resubmitted.
Brick went live. Epiphany's map search now uses Places; iPhone submitted. Curbfind shipped Near me and a smarter ranker; Apple rejected it and I replied. Conveyer retired the beaten world;

Tonchi gained Greek and Polish courses and chess puzzles.

NYC Survive's overnight loop made the game winnable and shipped to App Review (iOS live since 2026-10-06, macOS in review). Recruits tie to beds and food, squads take orders, and night raids come unless you set PATROL. A headless bot plays it to victory in about two hours, and the landing has real playthrough video.

Alex, Joshua's best-friend character, now runs on Samantha's local model.

## Oct 5 to 6

Swept 15 Apple Notes into project roadmaps and emptied Notes.app. Nimble shipped spoken math ("nine plus ten"), a local "who made you" answer on web and Swift, bigger full-contrast answers on web and Mac HUD, and changed the store name from "Nimble Answers" to "Nimble".

Blockframe dropped toolbar title and tan for neutral gray. Notate, Talli, Plank fixed UI (padding, scroll, GitHub URL). Joshua Tree added desktop button on hero.

## Oct 6

The Raspberry Pi 4 arrived and booted Joshua Tree at 1920x1080 (the CPU cache flush was the fix). I wrote the USB driver in QEMU first, and a keyboard typed on the Pi the moment I swapped cables. A Claude relay lets my Mac help build the OS from inside it.

## Oct 7

The Pi joined Wi-Fi. The chip has no login helper, so I wrote the WPA2 handshake myself. The stall that ate the most time was one detail: the router wanted a block of association data repeated exactly in my reply. Then a router reboot cleared a stretch of refused joins. The green light blinks on the real board again, four slow blinks.

Samantha answers on the real Pi through my API credit. A relay on my Mac picks a cheap or strong model for each question, so I do not choose. She can read a shared Mac folder, read only. I asked her my IP and how strong the Wi-Fi was, and she gave me the Pi's real address and signal. The SD card kept disconnecting, so flashing now waits, mounts and writes in one go.

An agent wrote the Pi-side actions on its own local branch. Asking Samantha now prints a note on the console and blinks the light. The relay on my Mac tells her about those actions. I also tried a Codex mode for the relay, and the safety check blocked it, correctly, since it would let anyone on the network run a program on my Mac. Codex on the Pi waits for a secure tunnel. I also had an overnight runner built for the repo, branches only, with a $5 cap per run, using Sonnet, Opus or Fable by task. The API spending limit is now $200.

Next: split the kernel's one giant C file, then HTTPS on the Pi. The BearSSL library in the project is only five crypto files, so the full TLS client has to be vendored, and the plan is written into the roadmap. Money comes from a Pi dev kit with a waitlist already live, and the OS stays free.

## Oct 8

I wrote the Joshua Tree story as an eleven-chapter book, with photos from the real board. The Pi's first benchmark: memory copies about 1100 MB/s, Wi-Fi in 6.4 seconds, clock in 8.7.

This afternoon the Pi got a calculator with scientific mode and a tiny language model that runs on the board itself. The browser sat on fetching because DNS was asking QEMU's address instead of the router. I fixed that, and this evening the Pi fetched heyitsmejosh.com over HTTPS and showed the title and a link. That is the first web page my own OS has loaded on the board. It cannot run scripts yet, so busy sites show little.

I also found a real hole. Every Pi image I built carried my relay key, not just the dev card. The fix is in review. Two more problems are on the list: the key goes over Wi-Fi in plain text, and the clock trusts an unchecked date header. Helper agents have spent about $133 of the $200 credit, roughly $66 left until November 4. Weekly usage is at 98 percent, so the loop waits for Saturday's reset.

## Oct 9

I halved my custom skills, merging overlap and keeping backups. The Pi had stranded me without a mouse because search would not close and I could not reach Terminal; I fixed both keyboard paths and caught the leftover search bar with a screen check. The SD card is flashed, checked and safely ejected, ready for another real-board test.
