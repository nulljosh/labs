## Oct 1 to 2

Joshua Tree reached its 2.0 release. Twenty-six apps now run in protected boxes, and I fixed the last flaky tests. Bookrank is live on iPhone with natural voices, Goodreads import, offline reading and highlights. Plank shipped a big update, and Conveyer launched a second rocket. All my apps are now 99 cents.

Epiphany got much faster. A map that took 23 seconds to load now takes under 4. Its Premium plan is $1, and its auto-trading feature is live. I also tested a stock rule called Double 7s on paper money: it buys after ten-day lows and sells after highs, and it won about 77 percent of the time on past data.

Talli was rejected because my test account did not work, so I fixed it and resubmitted. Healstack needed Sign in with Apple and was resubmitted. Notate went live on iPhone at 99 cents.

## Oct 3

Joshua Tree shipped six versions in one night. It now has a 30-second intro with my own cloned voice, and the face fills the screen with matching lip movement. Music and Movies apps work. Tests had been slow and a bit random, so I fixed how they split up, and they now take about 10 minutes. Turing can now answer as me as well as Samantha.

I started moving Joshua Tree onto Raspberry Pi hardware, the small cheap computer. The screen drawing already works. Everything else waits on a real device.

Fixes went out across several apps too. Windgate now shows minutes in plain words. Lexly was resubmitted with its new Pre-Calc 12 course. Notate's next version is ready. Every app got a legal setting that skips a form on first build.

## Oct 4

I am getting a Raspberry Pi 4B to test my operating system on real hardware. Until now it has only run inside a virtual computer, so this is the first time it will boot on an actual machine. The plan is first boot, then Wi-Fi, then the rest of the board. I will go slowly, because real hardware always behaves differently from the pretend version.

My idea-catching app Sparkjar went through two names today. I picked Hotaru, then found it is already on the App Store under another developer, so it is now Hikko. It also got a cleaner look, near-white paper with one terracotta accent, plus new screenshots and a daily 9am idea reminder. Version 3.0 is ready to send to Apple for iPhone and Mac. Pare became Hagaki, a Japanese postcard themed mail app, after about 150 name tries, and went to Apple for iPhone and Mac.

Turing can now send email, teach me things and explain code. I am training a bigger small AI for it. It scores better but makes more mistakes, so it stays off for now. The goal is under 10 wrong answers on my test set before I call it version 5.

## Apps

Joshua Tree, Bookrank, Epiphany, Plank, Nimble, Talli, Turing, Conveyer, Lexly, Notate, Hagaki, Hikko and Windgate. Next up: Raspberry Pi first boot.
