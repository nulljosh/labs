## Oct 1 to 2

Joshua Tree reached its 2.0 release. Twenty-six apps now run in protected boxes, and I fixed the last flaky tests. Bookrank is live on iPhone with natural voices, Goodreads import, offline reading and highlights. Plank shipped a big update, and Conveyer launched a second rocket. All my apps are now 99 cents.

Epiphany got much faster. A map that took 23 seconds to load now takes under 4. Its Premium plan is $1, and its auto-trading feature is live. I also tested a stock rule called Double 7s on paper money: it buys after ten-day lows and sells after highs, and it won about 77 percent of the time on past data.

Talli was rejected because my test account did not work, so I fixed it and resubmitted. Healstack needed Sign in with Apple and was resubmitted. Notate went live on iPhone at 99 cents.

## Oct 3

Joshua Tree shipped six versions in one night. It now has a 30-second intro with my own cloned voice, and the face fills the screen with matching lip movement. Music and Movies apps work. Tests had been slow and a bit random, so I fixed how they split up, and they now take about 10 minutes. Turing can now answer as me as well as Samantha.

I started moving Joshua Tree to Raspberry Pi hardware. Screen drawing works; everything else waits on the real device.

Fixes went out across several apps too. Windgate now shows minutes in plain words. Lexly was resubmitted with its new Pre-Calc 12 course. Notate's next version is ready. Every app got a legal setting that skips a form on first build.

## Oct 4

I am getting a Raspberry Pi 4B to test my operating system on real hardware. Until now it has only run inside a virtual computer, so this is the first time it will boot on an actual machine. The plan is first boot, then Wi-Fi, then the rest of the board. I will go slowly, because real hardware always behaves differently from the pretend version.

Joshua Tree had a big day. Apps on the Pi version now run in their own locked room, Bookrank, Tonchi and Hikko show real data from the real apps, and the Strata case is now called Neo. I spent the afternoon fixing what I spotted on my real monitor: Burrow cutting off file names, Stocks showing only one row, Mail and Weather losing the end of their files, and the menu bar vanishing after the Launchpad. The Launchpad redo is still in testing. The main branch stayed green all day, and my disk got close to full before I cleaned it up.

Madobe's landing loads any site through a sandboxed Worker proxy, stripping cookies and blocking outbound nav.

My idea-catching app got two names today. I picked Hotaru, then found it is already on the App Store under another developer, so it is now Hikko. It also got a cleaner look, near-white paper with one terracotta accent, and I fixed the daily idea generator so it stops posting near-copies (it was giving me five variants of "shared X tracker for roommates" in a row). Version 3.0 went to Apple for iPhone and Mac, and both are waiting for review. The iPhone build was pulled back once because the welcome screen, widgets and Face ID prompt still said Sparkjar or Spark; those all say Hikko now. The web app released with the new look. Mac 3.0 got terracotta in its sidebar and buttons, plus a new App Store screenshot. Pare became Hagaki, a Japanese postcard themed mail app, after about 150 name tries, and went to Apple for iPhone and Mac. Blockframe, my wireframe tool, still said Charblock in corners of the iPhone, watch and terminal apps; it says Blockframe everywhere now, App Store listing included, with a new build heading to Apple.

Lexly became Tonchi, my gamified learning app (quick wit in Japanese). Content exploded from 45 to 165 courses, including 120 new ones across programming, science, engineering, finance, and philosophy; every course was fact-checked. Lessons now open with a learn-first card, missed questions replay at the end. Web address moved to tonchi.heyitsmejosh.com, GitHub to nulljosh/tonchi, App Store listing updated. I tested 32 spoken language versions, found mistakes in all, kept 14. The Programming tab split into Programming, Computer Science, and Engineering. GitHub issues cleaned from 80 to 2. Footer merged, tests green.

Healstack got a major web redesign. The layout is now three tabs like Apple Health: Summary shows the most important metrics with tiny sparklines, Browse lists every substance, and Sharing exports or prints. New icon, white theme on light, blue accent on dark. I also fixed a privacy bug where new accounts were seeded with a hardcoded personal profile; they start empty now, and it is gone from the repo history. I am renaming it Intake: the folder and repo moved today, the store name follows. The iOS app was approved and is live on the App Store; the Mac version is still in review waiting on Apple's screenshot feedback.

Turing shipped ten incremental updates with a rebuilt landing page; demo plays all features with no buttons, plus a commercial in her voice ending with an "on a real Mac" card (research, screen reading, clicking) the web stand-in cannot show, so landing grades A. Fixed coder tool to ask before reading. Trained the one-point-five-billion picker on Kaggle; first DPO collapsed, so trainer scores itself and keeps the better one. Run beat dev-set leak bar but sealed heldout missed refusal goal at three-point-six percent. Joshua asked to ship version five; I declined because refusals hold at three percent on unseen sets, and five needs a fresh sealed set plus refusal fixes.

Brick, my real estate browser, went live with live listings from two sources. HousingFeed covers 30 countries and RentCast handles US sales and rentals; results cache for 24 hours in a KV namespace. An AI mode turns plain words into filters, and a deal score ranks homes by percent under typical for the same bedroom count. The iPhone app now fills the screen with a map under a Liquid Glass sheet. The landing page has a 27-second video shot in the real app, with feature cards and honest copy. Desktop and Android apps are building in CI, and tests are green. GitHub renamed the repo to nulljosh/brick and the product to Brick (new logo, neutral white and grey palette). The App Store record for Brick is pending; an earlier 503 on web sign-in kept it from being created, and the next retry is scheduled.

Bookrank shipped an update on iPhone. The app now fills missing book covers by looking them up on Open Library and saves them to the database, mirroring the web. I added three covers to books.json; seven still need images. The build went to App Review.
