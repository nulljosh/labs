## Oct 1 to 2

Joshua Tree reached its 2.0 release. Twenty-six apps now run in protected boxes, and I fixed the last flaky tests. Bookrank is live on iPhone with natural voices, Goodreads import, offline reading and highlights. Plank shipped a big update, and Conveyer launched a second rocket. All my apps are now 99 cents.

Epiphany got much faster. A map that took 23 seconds to load now takes under 4. Its Premium plan is $1, and its auto-trading feature is live. I also tested a stock rule called Double 7s on paper money: it buys after ten-day lows and sells after highs, and it won about 77 percent of the time on past data.

Talli was rejected because my test account did not work, so I fixed it and resubmitted. Healstack needed Sign in with Apple and was resubmitted. Notate went live on iPhone at 99 cents.

## Oct 3

Joshua Tree shipped six versions in one night. It now has a 30-second intro with my own cloned voice, and the face fills the screen with matching lip movement. Music and Movies apps work. Tests had been slow and a bit random, so I fixed how they split up, and they now take about 10 minutes. Turing can now answer as me as well as Samantha.

I started moving Joshua Tree onto Raspberry Pi hardware, the small cheap computer. The screen drawing already works. Everything else waits on a real device.

Fixes went out across several apps too. Windgate now shows minutes in plain words. Lexly was resubmitted with its new Pre-Calc 12 course. Notate's next version is ready. Every app got a legal setting that skips a form on first build.

## Oct 4

I am getting a Raspberry Pi 4B to test my operating system on real hardware. Until now it has only run inside a virtual computer, so this is the first time it will boot on an actual machine. The plan is first boot, then Wi-Fi, then the rest of the board. I will go slowly, because real hardware always behaves differently from the pretend version.

My idea-catching app got two names today. I picked Hotaru, then found it is already on the App Store under another developer, so it is now Hikko. It also got a cleaner look, near-white paper with one terracotta accent, and I fixed the daily idea generator so it stops posting near-copies (it was giving me five variants of "shared X tracker for roommates" in a row). Version 3.0 went to Apple for iPhone and Mac, and both are waiting for review. The iPhone build was pulled back once because the welcome screen, widgets and Face ID prompt still said Sparkjar or Spark; those all say Hikko now. The web app released with the new look. Mac 3.0 got terracotta in its sidebar and buttons, plus a new App Store screenshot. Pare became Hagaki, a Japanese postcard themed mail app, after about 150 name tries, and went to Apple for iPhone and Mac.

Lexly became Tonchi, my learning app with gamified courses across languages, code, and science. I picked the name myself from a short list (tonchi is Japanese for quick wit). The brand color switched from red to blue, the landing got shorter, and content exploded from 45 courses to 165. That included 12 old courses each checked by a stronger model, plus 120 new ones across programming languages, computer science, engineering, hardware, finance, investing, economics, psychology, philosophy and poker. Every new course was written by a cheaper model and fact-checked, which found dozens of mistakes per course (wrong answer keys, odds figures, compound interest). Lessons now open with a learn-first card, missed questions come back at the end, and every answer says what it means. Web address moved to tonchi.heyitsmejosh.com (the old one still works as an alias). GitHub went to nulljosh/tonchi. App Store listing pushed with the new name, subtitle "Learn anything, 5 min a day", and web address for privacy and support links; old name still shows on the store until the pending iOS review finishes. Sign-in redirects extended to work on the new host. I tested 32 generated spoken languages, found wrong sentences in every one, and pulled them back; 14 stayed. The Programming tab split into Programming, Computer Science, and Engineering. All 12 early courses now fact-checked by stronger model (130 total fixes across all checked courses). GitHub issues cleaned from 80 down to 2 (remaining: new app screenshots for the store once iOS ships, and a stray duplicate App Store record only Apple can delete). Footer directory branch merged, tests and deploys green. A placement test, weak-words practice button, and 165 courses are live.

Healstack got a major web redesign. The layout is now three tabs like Apple Health: Summary shows the most important metrics with tiny sparklines, Browse lists every substance, and Sharing exports or prints. New icon, white theme on light, blue accent on dark. Fixed desktop page overflow, removed the date heading, and squashed a few bugs. Most importantly, I fixed a privacy bug where new accounts were being seeded with a hardcoded personal profile; they start empty now. The iOS app was approved and is live on the App Store; the Mac version is still in review waiting on Apple's screenshot feedback.

Turing shipped nine incremental updates with tighter guard rules, a data fix, and a brand new landing page where the demo plays through every feature automatically with no buttons. Shot a ninety-four second commercial of the real thing in her own voice (A-minus: six of eight feature families visible, no music bed). Fixed the coder tool to register as a real tool and ask before reading. Trained the bigger one-point-five-billion picker model three times on Kaggle; the first DPO run collapsed, so the trainer now scores itself and keeps the better one. The third run read under the leak bar on dev sets but missed the refusal goal at three-point-six percent when under one percent was needed, so the next version did not ship. Fresh sealed test required.

Brick, my real estate browser, went live with live listings from two sources. HousingFeed covers 30 countries and RentCast handles US sales and rentals; results cache for 24 hours in a KV namespace. An AI mode turns plain words into filters, and a deal score ranks homes by percent under typical for the same bedroom count. The iPhone app now fills the screen with a map under a Liquid Glass sheet. The landing page has a 27-second video shot in the real app, with feature cards and honest copy. Desktop and Android apps are building in CI, and tests are green. GitHub renamed the repo to nulljosh/brick and the product to Brick (new logo, neutral white and grey palette). The App Store record for Brick is pending; an earlier 503 on web sign-in kept it from being created, and the next retry is scheduled.
