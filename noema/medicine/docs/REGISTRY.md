# ClinicalTrials.gov checks

These studies check posted results on ClinicalTrials.gov against themselves. `registry.py` reads baseline tables and `analyses.py` reads outcome analyses.

## Do registry Totals add up?

ClinicalTrials.gov posts a baseline table for every trial with results: each arm's count, mean and SD, then a Total column. `registry.py fetch` pulls every trial with results. 55,075 post a mean with an SD, and `registry.py` checks 71,214 Totals against their arms. None of the Totals falls outside the range of its arm means. The registry may check that itself.

The Total should be the mean weighted by arm size. 2,154 miss that by more than ten roundings. Missing values may explain many of them, because the posted counts are not always the people measured. But 328 Totals, in 234 trials, land to the last digit on the plain average of the arm means. That is right only when the arms are the same size, and luck would give about 27. A decoy test agrees. Take the point as far from the weighted mean as the plain average, on the other side. Missing data should push Totals to either side alike. Where both points fit inside the arm range, 283 Totals land on the plain average and 11 on the decoy. In 168 of the 328 the Total SD is also the plain average of the arm SDs and falls outside every pooled SD the arms allow. These look like Totals typed in as a spreadsheet AVERAGE of the arms.

To be safe, the clear set keeps only trials whose arms differ at least twofold in size and carry no note about missing data: 134 Totals in 107 trials, listed in `registry-baseline.json`. In one, arms of 8 and 18 people have mean BMIs of 27.7 and 33.53. The Total is 30.615, the plain average; weighted, it is 31.74.

Head counts behave. Across all 79,533 trials with results, every arm count adds up to its Total, 435,201 cells without one miss, so the registry likely adds count Totals itself while mean Totals are typed by hand. Sex, age group, race and ethnicity add past their arm's size in only 49 rows of 22 trials, and 41 of those are exactly double, the shape of a crossover trial that counts each person in both periods.

Total SDs can be checked without any counts. However the arms pool, with at least two people each, the Total's SD is at least 0.7 times the smallest arm SD and at most the largest arm SD widened by the spread of the arm means. `registry.py` flags a Total SD under half the smallest arm SD, or over twice the widest pooling allows, when the Total mean itself sits inside the arms. 407 of 70,923 such Totals, in 296 trials, fail, so no table like them can be right. 24 print an SD of 0. 42 sit near the arm SD divided by the square root of N, which is a standard error. 3 repeat the Total mean and 3 the head count. In others the arms look like the slip: arms of 11 and 12 people with VO2peak SDs of 1.0 and 0.9 beside a Total SD of 3.1. One trial gives ages of 43.6 and 31.9 with SDs of 9.5 and 10, then a Total SD of .17. Three flagged trials were fetched again live and match cell for cell. The rows are in `registry-sd.json`. The checker cannot say which cell is wrong, only that one is.

Two literature searches found studies of missing and inconsistent registry fields, but none that checks the Total column this way, and no sign of how the registry validates Totals.

## Do registry confidence intervals have the right signs?

Results pages also post analyses: an estimate, a 95% interval and often a p-value. `analyses.py fetch` pulls all 329,322 of them. 126,313 are differences (mean, LS mean, risk, slope) with a two-sided interval, and 119,013 of those sit symmetric about their estimate, as most such intervals do. Some that do not become symmetric when one limit changes sign, like a mean difference of -21.6 with an interval of -23.52 to 19.58, where -19.58 fits. Lopsided intervals can be honest, so the p-value decides. In 61 analyses, in 52 trials, the posted interval disagrees with the posted p about zero and the flipped one agrees: -21.6 (-23.52, 19.58) with p below 0.0001. In 28 the p sides with the posted interval instead, which is about how often luck makes a flip look right. Intervals symmetric on the log scale are ratios under a difference label and are left out. Three flagged trials were fetched again live and match cell for cell. The rows are in `registry-ci-sign.json`. In 39 a limit that should be negative is printed positive, most often the upper limit of a negative difference (31). In 22 a limit picks up a stray minus, usually copied from a negative estimate onto an upper limit that crosses zero (20). A literature search found registry-versus-paper discrepancy studies, and Wren and Georgescu (2018) checking ratios against intervals in abstracts, but none on sign slips in registry analyses.
