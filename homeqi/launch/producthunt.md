Description (<=260 chars):
Answer 24 questions and see what the book says is working against you. Every worrying answer shows the book's reasoning and its chapter. Covers land, neighborhood, building and objects. Condensed from Good Fengshui by Eva Wong. No account. Free.

Topics (3):
Lifestyle, Real Estate, Web App

First comment (maker story):
Good Fengshui by Eva Wong is useful, but it's long. It also never gives you a way to check the room you're standing in.

Here's what I noticed. Its chapters are already checklists. So Homeqi turns them into 24 questions across four layers, from the outside in: land, neighborhood, building and objects. You answer yes, no or unsure.

Every worrying answer shows the book's reasoning and links back to the chapter it came from. You don't have to take a score on trust. You can read the argument yourself.

The order is deliberate. Land and neighborhood come first, and they're marked as siting decisions. The book is blunt about it: nothing you do indoors fixes them. I kept that bluntness in the app, because the book puts it that way and it changes what the other answers mean.

Answers stay in your browser. There's no account and no backend. The chapter text is in the app too, so you can read it while you check the room.

The web is the product. The iOS app is parked. Questions, and any place you think the book is wrong, are welcome in the comments.
