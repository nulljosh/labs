First comment (maker story):
Nobody shows you the whole map. School hands you a few rooms and never the floor plan. You can finish a degree and still not know what topology is, or that the kidney sets your blood pressure, or that most physicists study materials.

That's the gap. Fieldbook is one page that covers all of it, every field of science and math, a few hundred words each.

Each entry says what the field studies, gives the four ideas that matter, and names the thing most people get wrong. It's plain language, so you can read it top to bottom in an afternoon.

An encyclopedia is a warehouse. This is the map on the wall by the door. That's the whole product.

It's free, with no account and no course. The next step is a question per field, so you can check that you got it. After that, the same treatment for anything else worth learning alone.
