# Photos and location data (Dad's question)

Dad asked: does Google like real photos better, with the original location and data? Short answer for him:

**Real photos: yes.** Google asks businesses to post photos that show the real business. His own job photos beat stock or AI images, and customers can tell. Fresh photos on the Google Business Profile every week or two is the habit that matters.

**Location data (the GPS tag inside the file): unproven.** Google has never said it ranks photos by it. John Mueller is reported to have called geotagging unnecessary for SEO. Independent tests disagree, and the most cited ones found no effect. Google also strips the metadata from photos once they are on a Business Profile, and Instagram and Facebook generally do too. So keeping it will not hurt, but do not count on it.

**What does help:**
- A caption or post that names the city and the job ("spring repair in Port Moody").
- A clear file name before uploading, like `port-moody-spring-repair.jpg`.
- The same real photos on the website pages, with alt text.
- Posting often.

**Privacy:** a job photo's GPS tag is a customer's home address. Public copies in this repo have the location stripped. Keep the untouched original from his phone for the Google Business Profile upload, and avoid showing house numbers or neighbours.

Sources: Search Engine Land, "Geotagging photos for Google Business Profile" (searchengineland.com/geotagging-photos-google-business-profile-rank-453525), and Whitespark, "Geotagging Photos is a Local SEO Myth" (whitespark.ca).
