I'm on disability in BC. Most days the thing I can do is build, so I built. Over about five months, with Claude Code doing a lot of the typing, twenty apps went live in the App Store. Three more never made it past review.

This is what that looked like, including the part where the money didn't show up.

## How it works

I decide what an app is and what it isn't. Claude writes most of the code. Then I make it prove itself: tests, a build, a look at the real screen. Nothing counts as shipped because it compiled. I've thrown away a lot of working code that was the wrong idea.

Every app gets a web version first, because a web page is the cheapest trial there is. The App Store is the checkout. Mac and iPhone come from the same SwiftUI code where I can manage it.

## What went wrong

In late August, Apple rejected seven apps in one wave under guideline 4.3(a), the spam rule. Twenty small apps from one developer looks like spam to a reviewer, and I understand why. I appealed. Curvely, Sparkjar and Healstack came back. Others are still sitting there, and I'm not resubmitting into it.

Three apps have never been live: Windgate, Siftbox and Madobe. I'd rather say that than leave them out.

## What I changed

Everything is free or a dollar now. Eight apps are 99 cents, the rest are free, and a few have a one dollar unlock on the web. No subscriptions. No tips. I don't want to run a business that depends on nagging people.

Each landing page runs the real app, so you can try it before you pay. The list of everything, with prices, is at heyitsmejosh.com/buy.html.

## Where it goes

October is the month I try to sell, honestly, in public. If a sale happens I'll say so. If none does, I'll say that too.
